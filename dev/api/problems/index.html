Problems and solves

The problem

A problem holds functions and, for a NonlinearProblem, an optional Jacobian prototype: the residual, optionally its derivative, and optionally jacobian_prototype for a sparse R3 Jacobian in NonlinearProblem; the residual, optionally its derivative, and its length in LeastSquaresProblem; or the scalar action, its equation and unknown index sets, and an optional datum in StationarityProblem. Reparametrized wraps a NonlinearProblem or a StationarityProblem in a coordinate chart x = g(y, p), and Deflated wraps either in a deflation of the roots it is given, so that a solve from the same start can converge to another root. BlockEliminated partitions the unknowns of a NonlinearProblem or a StationarityProblem into inner unknowns y = x[Y] and outer unknowns z = x[Z], eliminates y at each outer iterate by solving the inner equations, and steps on the outer equations with the Schur complement J_zz - J_zy J_yy⁺ J_yz; VariableProjection does the same for a LeastSquaresProblem across all m residual rows by Golub–Pereyra variable projection or Kaufman's reduced Jacobian (BlockEliminated on a LeastSquaresProblem forwards to VariableProjection). The storage of the iterate selects the call form of the functions, so one problem serves every regime. NonlinearProblem, StationarityProblem, LeastSquaresProblem, Reparametrized, Deflated, BlockEliminated and VariableProjection are exported; GeometricSolvers.jacobian_prototype and GeometricSolvers.jacobian_pattern are internal accessors.

GeometricSolvers.AbstractNonlinearProblem — Type
AbstractNonlinearProblem

The supertype of the problems a NonlinearSolver solves: NonlinearProblem, whose residual has as many entries as the iterate has variables, StationarityProblem, whose residual is a part of the gradient of a scalar action, and LeastSquaresProblem, whose residual may have more entries than the iterate has variables. init, solve, solve! and solve!! take it, and so does Newton with each of its globalizations, so one solver loop serves every problem type; Picard, the combinators Additive, Multiplicative, Repeat and Switch, and solve_batch! take a NonlinearProblem or a StationarityProblem, and a combinator raises an ArgumentError for a LeastSquaresProblem.

GeometricSolvers.NonlinearProblem — Type
NonlinearProblem(F; jacobian_prototype = nothing)
NonlinearProblem(F, J; jacobian_prototype = nothing)
NonlinearProblem(F, J; jacobian_prototype = nothing,
    block_residuals = nothing, block_jacobians = nothing)
NonlinearProblem(F, J; jacobian_prototype = nothing,
    stage_matrix = nothing, stage_jacobian = nothing)

The residual F of a nonlinear system, optionally its derivative J, and optionally a matrix jacobian_prototype giving the storage type and sparsity pattern of the Jacobian. With no prototype (jacobian_prototype = nothing), the problem holds functions only — no parameters, no iterate and no flag — so it is isbits when F and J are and a kernel can hold it.

With block_residuals given, the problem also carries one local residual F_i!(f_i, x_i, x, p) per block of a BlockPartition — writing the residual of block i on its own index set into f_i, reading external couplings from the frozen full iterate x — and optionally one block Jacobian J_i!(J_i, x_i, x, p) per block, and its F is the BlockResidual that holds them. J stays as the caller gives it; RAS reads the block functions and every other method the global ones. block_jacobians without block_residuals, and the two of different lengths, raise an ArgumentError.

With stage_matrix = A and stage_jacobian = jac! given, F is the residual Z - h (A ⊗ I) f(yₙ + Z) of the stage system of an implicit Runge–Kutta method in the stacked stage increments Z, the problem's F is the StageResidual that holds the three, and KroneckerFactorization solves its Newton steps. jac!(J, Z, p) writes the n × n Jacobian of the vector field into J, at a point that the caller chooses from Z and p, and the step h is read as p.h. One of the two without the other, and either with block_residuals or block_jacobians, raise an ArgumentError.

The call form follows the storage of the iterate, and residual!! selects it on the storage alone: with no buffer slot the call is F(x, p), for a Number, an MVector — which counts as a value — and an Array alike. J is the derivative of F, which jvp!! and jacobian!! read for the source nothing; AutoForwardDiff instead differentiates F. A method's own operator is a separate Jacobian source. When jacobian_prototype is a SparseMatrixCSC, Newton allocates its Jacobian buffer and linear factorisation from that pattern and AutoSparse uses its sparsity pattern directly. Its Jacobian then comes from an analytic J or an AutoSparse jacobian: AutoForwardDiff(), which is the source of Newton(; jacobian = nothing) without J, writes a dense Jacobian only, so Newton with it and a sparse prototype raises ArgumentError when it initialises. A linear solver exists for a host Vector iterate with a SparseMatrixCSC{<:Any, Int} prototype in two configurations: a real iterate with Sparspak loaded and an LUFactorization in the iterate's element type, and a Float64 or ComplexF64 iterate with UMFPACKFactorization; a QRFactorization, an SVDFactorization and a dense prototype raise MethodError when Newton initialises, and ArgumentError with UMFPACKFactorization. With CUDA and CUDSS loaded, a CuVector iterate with a CuSparseMatrixCSR prototype and an LUFactorization in its element type has the cuDSS factor. On a device iterate with a SparseMatrixCSC prototype, Newton(; jacobian = nothing) with an analytic J raises MethodError from the factor, or ArgumentError with UMFPACKFactorization, and an AutoSparse jacobian raises ArgumentError in prepare_ad first, as any back end other than AutoForwardDiff() does. Picard and a static or batched iterate ignore the prototype.

julia> prob.F(3.0, nothing)
5.0

julia> prob.J === nothing && prob.jacobian_prototype === nothing
true

GeometricSolvers.StationarityProblem — Type
StationarityProblem(S; E = :, U = :, b = nothing)
StationarityProblem(S, E, U, b = nothing)

A stationarity problem (∇S(x, p))_E + b = 0 for the unknown entries x[U] of a state x, with scalar action S(x, p), stationarity-equation index set E, unknown index set U, and optional datum b.

The residual and its Jacobian are

\[F_a(u, p) = \frac{\partial S}{\partial x_{E_a}}(x(u), p) + b_a, \qquad J_{a, c}(u, p) = \frac{\partial^2 S}{\partial x_{E_a}\,\partial x_{U_c}}(x(u), p), \qquad a, c \in \{1, \dots, |E|\},\]

where $x(u)_i = u_c$ for $i = U_c$ and $x(u)_i = (x_0)_i$ for $i \notin U$. The action S(x, p) is never used as a line-search or trust-region merit function: the merit is always $\|F\|_2^2 = \|(\nabla S)_E + b\|_2^2$, because stationarity points of physical actions include saddle points where $S$ is indefinite.

Where E == U, J is a block of the Hessian of S and is symmetric. For other index sets it is not symmetric in general, so LDLTFactorization, which reads only the lower triangle, does not apply, and LUFactorization or QRFactorization does.

The state x must be real. For a complex state the default automatic differentiation raises an error: an ArgumentError from ForwardDiff for an array iterate, and a MethodError for an R1 iterate. A StationarityProblem has no implicit differentiation: init, solve, solve! and solve_batch! raise an ArgumentError for a ForwardDiff.Dual in the iterate or in p.

Arguments

  • S: a scalar callable S(x, p) returning a scalar in the element type of x.
  • E: : (all indices 1:length(x₀)) or a duplicate-free collection of positive integer indices (Tuple, SVector, AbstractUnitRange, or AbstractVector{<:Integer}). A Tuple of integers is converted to SVector{N, Int} so an R1 problem is isbits. An R1 iterate (a StaticVector or a Number) takes :, a Tuple or an SVector, and any other index set raises an ArgumentError at the solve.
  • U: : or a duplicate-free collection of positive integer indices of the same length as E. E and U are both : or both index sets.
  • b: nothing (zero datum), a vector of length |E|, a Number, or a callable b(p) returning a vector of length |E| or a Number. The keyword b on init, solve, solve! and solve_batch! overrides prob.b for that solve; in solve_batch! a PerSystem b gives system i the datum b.data[i].

LeastSquaresProblem(F)

LeastSquaresProblem(F, J = nothing; output_length = nothing)

A nonlinear least-squares problem $\min_x \tfrac{1}{2}\|F(x, p)\|_2^2$ with residual F of length $m$ in $n$ variables ($m \ge n$) and optional Jacobian J.

For out-of-place problems (SVector or Number), $m$ is inferred from F(x, p) and the field output_length may be nothing. For in-place problems (F(r, x, p) on mutable arrays), output_length is required and must satisfy m >= 1 and m >= length(x).

A LeastSquaresProblem holds no Jacobian prototype, so its Jacobian is dense. It has no implicit differentiation: solve with a ForwardDiff.Dual in x₀ or p raises an ArgumentError, as init and solve! do for every problem.

GeometricSolvers.Reparametrized — Type
Reparametrized(prob, g, ∂g = nothing;
               covariant = prob isa StationarityProblem, curvature = true)

Coordinate-chart wrapper x = g(y, p) around an AbstractNonlinearProblem prob (NonlinearProblem, StationarityProblem or LeastSquaresProblem).

Solving a Reparametrized problem iterates in the chart coordinates y. solve and init / solve! return (x*, status) and solve!! returns (x*, status, state), where x* = g(y*, p) is evaluated at the returned chart iterate y*; solve_batch! writes each system's x* into the batch. For a StationarityProblem, the keyword b of init and solve sets the datum of the inner problem, as it does without the chart; with any other inner problem, b raises an ArgumentError.

Arguments and keywords

  • prob: the inner NonlinearProblem, StationarityProblem or LeastSquaresProblem in x-coordinates. For a LeastSquaresProblem, the rate test of the solve reads no step along a reused factor, as without the chart. A Reparametrized inner problem raises an ArgumentError: compose the two charts into one. A StationarityProblem needs U = : or U = 1:n in order, for the chart dimension n, and E = : or a permutation of 1:n; init, solve, solve_batch! and prepare_ad raise an ArgumentError for any other index set. For an R1 iterate the index sets are :, a tuple or an SVector, as for a StationarityProblem without a chart, so a range such as 1:n raises an ArgumentError there.
  • g: the chart map x = g(y, p) in R1 (StaticVector or Number) or g!(x, y, p) in R3 (AbstractVector).
  • ∂g = nothing: optional chart Jacobian G = ∂g(y, p) in R1 or ∂g!(G, y, p) in R3. When ∂g === nothing, G(y, p) = ∂g/∂y is computed by forward-mode automatic differentiation.
  • covariant::Bool: when false (default for NonlinearProblem), the transformed residual and Jacobian are $\tilde{F}(y, p) = F(g(y, p), p)$ and $\tilde{J}(y, p) = J(g(y, p), p) G(y, p)$. When true (default for StationarityProblem), the residual is pulled back covariantly as $\tilde{F}(y, p) = G(y, p)^\top F(g(y, p), p)$.
  • curvature::Bool = true: when covariant == true, includes the chart-curvature term $(\partial G^\top / \partial y) F(g(y, p), p)$ in $\tilde{J}(y, p) = G^\top J G + (\partial G^\top / \partial y) F$ when true, or uses the pull-back approximation $\tilde{J}(y, p) = G^\top J G$ when false.

GeometricSolvers.Deflated — Type
Deflated(prob, roots; power = 2, shift = 1)
Deflated(prob, roots, power, shift)

Shifted norm deflation of the known roots roots around NonlinearProblem or StationarityProblem prob: the problem whose residual is

\[F_{\mathrm{def}}(x) = M(x) F(x), \qquad M(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{k} \left( \|x - r_i\|_2^{-p} + \sigma \right),\]

and whose Jacobian is the product rule of that product,

\[J_{\mathrm{def}}(x) = M(x) J(x) + F(x) \nabla M(x)^\top,\]

so that, for p > 1/2, the known simple roots r₁, …, r_k repel the full steps of Newton started from the same x₀ (Farrell, Birkisson and Funke 2015, Deflation techniques for finding distinct solutions of nonlinear partial differential equations). Near a simple root rᵢ, a Newton step on the deflated residual multiplies x - rᵢ by about -p/(1-p) for p ≠ 1, whose modulus exceeds one for p > 1/2, and leaves the neighbourhood of rᵢ for p = 1, which is what pushes the iterate away from it. A known root that is not simple can still attract the solve, and so can a simple one under a line search, which shortens the step that overshoots it: with p = 3/4, F(x) = x^2 deflated at 0 from x₀ = 1/2, and F(x) = x deflated at 0 from x₀ = 0.1 with Backtracking, both return SUCCESS near 0. Deflation does not guarantee that another root exists or that the solve reaches one either: F(x) = x - 1 deflated at 1 from x₀ = 0.6 ends with MAXITERS. The caller reads the returned status and compares the solution with the known roots.

The steps and the line search take the deflated residual, but the stopping test of Options reads the inner one: status.fnorm is ‖F(x)‖, f_abstol bounds it, and f_reltol is relative to ‖F(x₀)‖, so a deflated root meets the tolerance of a solve of prob.

power (p > 1/2; p ≤ 1/2 raises an ArgumentError, because a solve with it can return SUCCESS even at a known simple root under full steps) and shift (σ > 0) are stored in T = float(real(eltype(first(roots)))), so Float32 roots give power::Float32 and a Float32 product; a solve needs an iterate of that element type and a real one.

Roots

roots is a non-empty Tuple of roots — a Number for a Number iterate, an SVector for an R1 iterate, an array of the iterate's own back end for an R3 iterate (a Vector for a Vector, a JLArray or an MtlArray for one on that device) — or an AbstractVector of real vectors. Every root has the length of the iterate and finite real entries, and the roots of one set share their element type and are all numbers or all vectors. A solve on an array iterate raises an ArgumentError for a root on another back end or a static root, because the deflation broadcasts x .- r on the iterate's back end. The AbstractVector form is not isbits, so solve_batch! refuses it: the batch kernel takes the problem by value.

For a wrapped StationarityProblem a root is a full state, as solve returns it, and the distance is taken on the unknowns x_U: M and ∇M are functions of the |U| entries the solve steps on, so ∇M has length |U| whatever the length of the state.

Evaluation

At a deflated root (x = rᵢ) the factor mᵢ is Inf, so a solve from x₀ = rᵢ returns NONFINITE at 0 iterations, and a trial step onto rᵢ has a non-finite merit, which Backtracking shrinks away from by the step min(0.1α, √α √αmin). A factor that overflows (‖x - rᵢ‖^{-p} = Inf, as at 10^{-200} in Float64) does the same. Every coordinate of ∇mᵢ is evaluated unit-scaled, -(p ((uⱼ/d) ϕ)/d) with d = ‖u‖ and ϕ = d^{-p}, which forms no ϕ/d² and so leaves no NaN where uⱼ is zero: the unscaled -(p ϕ/d²) uⱼ overflows to Inf at d = 10^{-100} and gives Inf * 0.0 = NaN there. Where ‖u‖^{-p-1} overflows although ‖u‖^{-p} does not (between about 10^{-155} and 10^{-103} in Float64 for p = 2), ∇mᵢ has an infinite coordinate and the Jacobian is not finite.

A Newton with a callable jacobian raises an ArgumentError, because Newton would take it as the deflated Jacobian. Give an analytic Jacobian of F to the wrapped NonlinearProblem instead.

Implicit differentiation

solve on a Deflated(NonlinearProblem) with a ForwardDiff.Dual in x₀ or p runs the primal deflated solve and differentiates the undeflated prob implicitly at the root it found, as solve does on the problem itself. A Deflated(StationarityProblem) refuses a dual with an ArgumentError, as a StationarityProblem does.

R2

solve_batch! solves every system of a batch under one Deflated, so each system is deflated at the same roots. The batch of a wrapped StationarityProblem takes the datum of its solves from the keyword b, shared or PerSystem, or from the problem's own b.

julia> prob = NonlinearProblem((x, p) -> x^3 - x);

julia> def = Deflated(prob, (0.0,));

julia> solve(def, 0.1, Newton())[1] ≈ 1 || solve(def, 0.1, Newton())[1] ≈ -1
true

julia> solve(Deflated(prob, (0.0,)), 0.0, Newton())[2].code === NONFINITE
true

GeometricSolvers.BlockEliminated — Type
BlockEliminated(prob, inner; outer = nothing, inner_eqs = inner, outer_eqs = outer,
                inner_method = Newton(; linsolve = SVDFactorization()),
                linear_inner = false, inner_options = nothing)

Partition the unknowns x of a NonlinearProblem or StationarityProblem prob into inner indices Y = inner (y = x[Y]) and outer indices Z = outer (z = x[Z], defaulting to the complement of inner in 1:n), and the residual equations into inner equations E_y = inner_eqs and outer equations E_z = outer_eqs (|E_y| == |Y|, |E_z| == |Z|). The default outer_eqs = outer takes the outer unknown indices as equation indices, so give outer_eqs whenever inner_eqs differs from inner. On a LeastSquaresProblem, BlockEliminated(prob, inner; kwargs...) forwards to VariableProjection.

At each outer iterate z, the inner equations $F_{E_y}(y, z) = 0$ are solved for $y^*(z)$: in one linear solve $y^*(z) = -J_{yy}(0, z)^+ F_{E_y}(0, z)$ when linear_inner = true, or from the previous inner solution y_warm when linear_inner = false, with the adaptive tolerance $\tau_{\text{inner}} = \max(10\,\varepsilon\max(1, \|F_{E_y}^0\|_2), \min(c, 0.5 \|F_{E_z}(\text{prev})\|_2^2))$, where $c$ is the tolerance cap of inner_options (unbounded when they are nothing) and $\|F_{E_y}^0\|_2$ is the inner residual at the warm start. The cap stands alone before the first outer residual exists, so the tolerance is $\max(10\,\varepsilon\max(1, \|F_{E_y}^0\|_2), c)$ with inner_options and the floor alone without them. The inner iteration is Newton's method with step halving (at most 8 halvings per step) and at most max_iterations steps of inner_options (50 without them); inner_method supplies only the linear solver of these steps and of the Schur complement (inner_method.linsolve for a Newton, or a linear method itself). The inner iteration reads only f_abstol, f_reltol and max_iterations of inner_options, against the 2-norm, so their norm has no effect. The outer solver then steps on the reduced residual $F_{\text{red}}(z) = F_{E_z}(\tilde y(z), z)$, where for linear_inner = false and an inner residual above the floor $\tilde y(z) = y - J_{yy}(y, z)^+ F_{E_y}(y, z)$ is the inner iterate y refined by one Newton correction (it agrees with $F_{E_z}(y^*(z), z)$ to first order in $F_{E_y}$), and otherwise $\tilde y = y$; the outer Jacobian is the Schur complement

\[S(z) = J_{zz}(y^*(z), z) - J_{zy}(y^*(z), z)\, J_{yy}(y^*(z), z)^+\, J_{yz}(y^*(z), z).\]

At return, $x^*[Y] = y^*(z^*)$ and $x^*[Z] = z^*$. Any inner solver failure or rank-deficient inner Jacobian (fac_yy.rank < |Y| under SVD) increments status.step_failures. The linear solve of linear_inner = true solves the inner equations only when they are affine in y: an inner residual $\|F_{E_y}(y^*(z), z)\|_2$ above $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\max(1, \|F_{E_y}(0, z)\|_2)$ also counts as an inner failure, and the solve goes on.

GeometricSolvers.VariableProjection — Type
VariableProjection(prob::LeastSquaresProblem, inner; outer = nothing,
                   linsolve = SVDFactorization(), jacobian_mode = :kaufman,
                   linear_inner = true,
                   inner_method = GaussNewton(; linsolve = SVDFactorization()),
                   inner_options = nothing)

Golub–Pereyra variable projection for a LeastSquaresProblem prob, partitioning the unknowns x into inner (linear or fast) parameters Y = inner (y = x[Y]) and outer parameters Z = outer (z = x[Z], defaulting to the complement of inner in 1:n) across all m residual rows.

For each outer iterate z, the inner subproblem $\min_y \tfrac{1}{2}\|r(y, z)\|_2^2$ is solved for $y^*(z)$ (in one linear least-squares solve $y^*(z) = -\Phi(z)^+ r(0, z)$ with $\Phi(z) = \partial r / \partial y$ when linear_inner = true, or by a Gauss–Newton iteration with step halving, warm-started from y_warm, when linear_inner = false). linsolve factorises $\Phi(z)$ when linear_inner = true; with linear_inner = false the linear solver of inner_method (inner_method.linsolve for a Newton, or a linear method itself) does, in the iteration and in the reduced Jacobian. The iteration reads the tolerances and max_iterations of inner_options as BlockEliminated does, against the 2-norm, so their norm has no effect. The outer solver steps on the reduced residual $r_{\text{red}}(z) = r(y^*(z), z)$ with reduced Jacobian (m × |Z|):

  • jacobian_mode = :kaufman (default): Kaufman's first-term reduced Jacobian $J_{\text{red}}(z) = (I - \Phi(z)\Phi(z)^+) D(z)$, where $D(z) = \partial r(y^*(z), z)/\partial z$.
  • jacobian_mode = :golub_pereyra: the full Golub–Pereyra reduced Jacobian $J_{\text{red}}(z) = (I - \Phi(z)\Phi(z)^+) D(z) - (\Phi(z)^+)^H (\partial\Phi/\partial z)^H r_{\text{red}}(z)$.

Any inner solver failure or rank-deficient $\Phi$ increments status.step_failures. The linear solve of linear_inner = true minimises over y only when r is affine in y: an inner gradient $\|\Phi(y^*)^H r(y^*, z)\|_2$ above $\sqrt{\varepsilon}\max(1, \|r(0, z)\|_2)\max(1, \|\Phi(y^*)\|_F)$ also counts as an inner failure, and the solve goes on.

Reduced-space elimination with a batch inside the residual

BatchEliminated eliminates a whole batch of independent inner systems from the residual of an outer matrix-free Newton, rather than a block of one unknown vector: the outer solver steps on the m-vector alone, and every evaluation of that residual — the Krylov products of the outer step included — solves the batch and deposits its moments. It is the pattern of the implicit PIC method (Chen2011, pp. 7025 and 7033; LanzkronRoseWilkes:1996), where the outer Newton works on the field and the per-particle orbit solves are the eliminated unknowns. The inner tolerance of each evaluation is the adaptive quadratic $\max(2\varepsilon(R_{\text{in}}), R_{\text{in}}(\tfrac{1}{2}\min(c, \|g_2(\text{prev})\|^2)/\sqrt{N_p}))$, the batch is warm-started from the previous evaluation — so a second evaluation at the same iterate and tolerance takes 0 inner iterations — and the outer Jacobian–vector product is a one-sided difference at the step $\varepsilon \max(1, \|x\|)/\|v\|$, with the relative step $\varepsilon = \sqrt{\operatorname{eps}(R_{\text{eff}})}$ of the coarser of the two precisions, so that a Float32 batch inside a Float64 outer solve converges at the tolerance its own accuracy supports and, below it, ends on a stall rather than on a factorisation failure — on the problem of make_pic_elimination_problem, the solve at 2e-4 returns SUCCESS and the one at 1e-10 STALLED. The batch's BatchSummary reports its global residual norm, whether every system converged, and how many did not; deposited_moments returns what the deposit wrote, and reset_batch! returns the problem to its initial state. make_pic_elimination_problem builds the one-dimensional implicit electrostatic PIC test problem that the elimination checks solve.

GeometricSolvers.BatchEliminated — Type
BatchEliminated(outer_F!, inner_prob, Y₀, deposit!, moments; per_system = nothing,
                inner_method = Newton(), inner_options = nothing,
                inner_atol_cap = 1e-2, eliminated_indices = nothing,
                workgroupsize = 64)

Reduced-space nonlinear elimination with an R2 batch inside the outer residual: the outer solver steps on the m-vector that outer_F! returns, and every evaluation of that residual — the Jacobian–vector products of a matrix-free outer Newton included — solves the whole batch of length(Y₀) independent inner systems and deposits its moments.

inner_prob is the NonlinearProblem of one inner system, solved for every system by solve_batch! from the batch's warm start Y, which the problem keeps in place across outer evaluations; Y₀ is the initial batch state and is never mutated, and Y is a host Vector copy of it, so the batch runs on the CPU backend whatever the array type of Y₀; per_system is the indexable per-system data of the batch, or nothing for a batch whose systems share all their parameters. deposit!(moments, Y, shared, per_system) fills moments from the batch Y and the shared parameter, and outer_F!(F_out, x, moments, shared) writes the outer residual at x — where shared is the outer iterate converted to real(eltype(eltype(Y₀))), which is what every system of the batch gathers. eliminated_indices are the indices of the eliminated unknowns in the stacked unknown vector, one for each entry of every system, 1:sum(length, Y₀) by default; a set of another length is a DimensionMismatch.

The inner tolerance of the batch at one outer evaluation is the adaptive quadratic one of LanzkronRoseWilkes:1996 (Thm 5.4 and Cor 5.5),

\[f_{\text{abstol},p} = \max\left(2\varepsilon(R_{\text{in}}), R_{\text{in}}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\min(\texttt{inner\_atol\_cap}, \|g_2(\text{prev})\|^2) / \sqrt{N_p}\right)\right),\]

so that the batch residual satisfies $\|g_1\| < \|g_2(\text{prev})\|^2$ wherever $\|g_2(\text{prev})\|^2 > 4\,\varepsilon(R_{\text{in}})\sqrt{N_p}$. Below that bound the floor binds: no arithmetic in R_in reaches the quadratic, and the batch residual stops at the floor of the batch's own precision instead of following the schedule. The inner solves take the step test, the iteration bounds, the norm and the round-off stop of inner_options, and no rate test (rate_test = false in their Options): the rate test ends a solve at a residual above the inner tolerance, so a batch converges only by the residual test. Each evaluation records the pair (‖g₁‖, ‖g₂(prev)‖) in the fields g₁_history and g₂_prev_history, with Inf before the first outer residual exists, the index set it eliminated in eliminated_index_log, and accumulates its inner counts in statuses. A batch whose systems did not all converge, with a residual above the coarse ceiling $32\,\varepsilon(R_{\text{in}})\sqrt{N_p}$, has no value to deposit: the residual of that evaluation is NaN and the outer solve cannot step on it. Under the ceiling the batch has stopped at the floor of its own arithmetic instead, and its deposit stands. The next evaluation takes the cap as its tolerance when the previous residual is not finite, since such a residual narrows nothing. reset_batch! returns the batch to Y₀ and the records to empty.

A second evaluation at the same (x, tolerance) reports 0 inner iterations for every system: the batch state is the root of the first one, and residual!!(F_out, prob, x, p; repeat_same_tol = true) re-uses the tolerance of the previous evaluation instead of narrowing it.

The outer Jacobian–vector product is a one-sided difference with the step $h = \varepsilon \max(1, \|x\|)/\|v\|$, scaled by the iterate and the direction, and the relative step $\varepsilon = \sqrt{\operatorname{eps}(R_{\text{eff}})}$, R_eff the coarser of the batch's and the outer iterate's element type (ChenChaconBarnes:2012, p. 5384): a Float32 batch inside a Float64 outer solve converges at the outer tolerance that the batch's accuracy supports and stalls, rather than failing, below it. Both evaluations of the difference are solved at the batch's own floor 2 eps(R_in), so the difference is the field's response and not the tolerance's, and the batch returns to prob.Y_anchor afterwards. The outer Newton takes jacobian = nothing or AutoForwardDiff() for this difference, or a JVPFunction of its own, and a GMRES without a jvp_eltype; any other back end, and a jvp_eltype, is an ArgumentError.

prob.Y, prob.Y_anchor, prob.statuses, prob.moments, the two history vectors and prob.eliminated_index_log are the state of the elimination, and a Jacobian–vector product writes its per-system statuses to prob.probe_statuses rather than to prob.statuses; eliminated_indices, deposited_moments, batch_summary, reset_batch! and make_pic_elimination_problem read and reset it.

Status and stopping test

Every type in this section is isbits, so a kernel can hold it, and every real field is in R = real(T) for the element type T of the iterate. SolverStatus also carries the range and spectrum diagnostics (rank, nullnorm and gradratio) recorded at the iterate $x_k$ of the last fresh Newton factorisation, not at the iterate $x$ at return (a solve that steps only along a factor kept with keep_factor = true reports the rank of that factor and NaN for the other two); a solve of a LeastSquaresProblem that ends on the stationarity test has factorised afresh at the iterate it returns. Where such a solve returns the start point of a step it discards, the status carries the diagnostics of the factorisation at that point, not those of the later re-measure at the end of the step; its iterations counts the steps that reached that point, and its stepnorm is that of the last of them. With regularization = μ ≠ 0, rank and nullnorm describe $J(x_k) + μ I$ and gradratio describes $J(x_k)$, so the classification below holds for μ = 0 only. For n ≥ 2 (for n = 1, a nonzero scalar Jacobian always gives gradratio = 1, and rank = 1 with an SVD factor), they classify the outcome and a STALLED or MAXITERS return: a consistent rank-deficient system that converged has a small residual (fnorm small) and nothing outside the range (rank < n, nullnorm ≈ 0); a least-squares point with no root has gradratio ≈ 0 (fnorm > 0) and the residual outside the range ($\text{nullnorm} \approx \|F(x_k)\| > 0$, and rank < n for a square system; a LeastSquaresProblem with more residuals than unknowns can have full rank there); a stall in nearly singular directions has the residual inside the range (nullnorm ≈ 0) at a fine SVD cutoff and outside it (rank < n, $\text{nullnorm} \approx \|F(x_k)\|$) at a coarse one; and an in-range stall from conditioning or step acceptance has gradratio bounded away from zero with nullnorm ≈ 0.

The residual test of Options reads the 2-norm of the residual, or with norm = MaxNorm() its max norm; status.fnorm is the 2-norm under either. L2Norm and MaxNorm are exported, and their supertype GeometricSolvers.StoppingNorm is not.

Each step's rate $θ_k = \|Δ_k\| / \|Δ_{k-1}\|$ goes into status.rate. The rate test of Options joins the step test: a step with $\hat θ_k = \max(θ_k, θ_{k-1}) < 1$ and $\hat θ_k / (1 - \hat θ_k) \|Δ_k\| ≤ κ \max(x_{abstol}, x_{reltol} \|x\|)$ returns SUCCESS with status.contracted set, and stopping_test then returns :contraction; it reads no step that a line search shortened below the full step, no step of a Multiplicative or a Repeat whose sub-steps cancel, so that the step is shorter than its longest sub-step, no step along an SVD factor whose rank is below the number of the unknowns it solves for, the outer ones of an eliminated problem, nor a step of a composition whose stepping member has such a factor. On a LeastSquaresProblem, a VariableProjection and a Reparametrized problem around a LeastSquaresProblem it reads no step along a reused factor, whose steps contract onto a point that need not be stationary. With rate_test = false no step reads the rate test, which the inner solves of a BatchEliminated problem use. With roundoff = true, a step whose increment stops decreasing returns STALLED at the iterate it reached. status.reused is set where the last step of a Newton solve with reuse stepped along a reused factor.

GeometricSolvers.SolverStatus — Type
SolverStatus{R <: Real}

The result of a solve. It is isbits, so a kernel can write one per system, and every real field is in R = real(T) for the element type T of the iterate.

Fields

  • code::ReturnCode: why the solve ended.
  • f_calls::Int16: the evaluations of the residual F of the solve, saturated at typemax(Int16); 0 for a method that keeps no count. A Newton with a matrix-free linear method (matrix_free) keeps the count, and so does a Repeat of it. The other combinators keep none and report 0: the members of a step-level combinator count only their own steps, and Fallback does not add the counts of the solves of its members.
  • iterations::Int32: the number of steps taken; for a solve that returns the start point of a step it discards, the steps that reached that point.
  • fnorm::R: $\|F(x)\|$ at return.
  • stepnorm::R: $\|Δx\|$ of the last step; for a solve that returns the start point of a step it discards, that of the step that reached that point.
  • step_failures::Int32: every step-rule failure of the solve, such as a failed line search, in one count.
  • promoted::Bool: a factorisation ran in a higher precision than the factorisation precision of its method, after a stall or as a vendor fallback.
  • stationary::Bool: the solve converged by the stationarity test rather than the residual test.
  • jvp_calls::Int16: the Jacobian–vector products of the solve, saturated at typemax(Int16); 0 for a method that keeps no count, the same methods as for f_calls.
  • rank::Int32: the numerical rank of the last fresh factorisation where it is an SVD factor with info == 0, else Int32(-1): no factorisation, an LU or QR factor, or a failed SVD factor.
  • nullnorm::R: $\|(I - U_r U_r^H) F(x_k)\|$ at the iterate $x_k$ of the last fresh factorisation, with $U_r$ the first rank columns of $U$, where rank is not Int32(-1), else R(NaN).
  • gradratio::R: $\|J(x_k)^H F(x_k)\| / (\|J(x_k)\|_F \|F(x_k)\|)$ at the iterate $x_k$ of the last fresh factorisation, zero(R) when $\|J(x_k)\|_F = 0$ or $\|F(x_k)\| = 0$, or R(NaN) when no Jacobian was factorised.
  • rate::R: the rate $θ_k = \|Δ_k\| / \|Δ_{k-1}\|$ of the last step $Δ_k$ against the step before it, which the rate test of Options reads; R(NaN) after 0 or 1 steps and after a step that followed a step of zero norm.
  • contracted::Bool: the solve converged by the rate test of Options.
  • reused::Bool: the last step of a Newton solve with reuse = k stepped along a factor that an earlier step factorised, of this solve or, with keep_factor = true, of an earlier one; for a GMRES with a preconditioner, along a preconditioner that an earlier step refreshed.

The three numbers are those of the last fresh factorisation ($x_k$), not of the iterate $x$ at return, and a solve that steps only along a factor kept with keep_factor = true reports the rank of that factor and NaN for the other two; a solve of a LeastSquaresProblem that ends on the stationarity test has factorised afresh at the iterate it returns, so its numbers are that iterate's. Where such a solve returns the start point of a step it discards, the numbers are those of the factorisation at that point, the iterate at return, and not those of the later re-measure at the end of the step. With regularization = μ ≠ 0, rank and nullnorm are those of the factored matrix $J(x_k) + μ I$ and gradratio is that of $J(x_k)$, so the classification below holds for μ = 0 only. The predicates GeometricSolvers.isconverged and GeometricSolvers.isstalled read the code. They are not exported, because they are generic names that a package doing using GeometricSolvers may want for itself.

GeometricSolvers.Options — Type
Options{R <: Real, N <: StoppingNorm}

The stopping test of the loop, and nothing else. It is isbits, and every tolerance is in R = real(T) for the element type T of the iterate, so a Float64 tolerance never reaches a Float32 kernel. The iteration cap of a line search is a field of the line search.

Fields

  • f_abstol::R, f_reltol::R: the residual test $\|F(x)\|_N ≤ \max(f_{abstol}, f_{reltol} \|F(x₀)\|_N)$ in the norm N.
  • x_abstol::R, x_reltol::R: the step test $\|Δx\|_2 ≤ \max(x_{abstol}, x_{reltol} \|x\|_2)$.
  • min_iterations::Int32, max_iterations::Int32: the bounds on the number of steps.
  • max_stalls::Int32: the number of consecutive stalled steps after which the solve stops.
  • g_tol::R: the stationarity tolerance $\|J^\top F\|_2 / (\|J\|_F \|F\|_2) \le g_{\text{tol}}$ on a LeastSquaresProblem.
  • norm::N: the StoppingNorm of the residual test, L2Norm or MaxNorm. It is a singleton, so Options has the size of its other fields.
  • kappa::R: the factor $κ ∈ (0, 1)$ of the rate test $\hat θ_k / (1 - \hat θ_k) \|Δ_k\|_2 ≤ κ \max(x_{abstol}, x_{reltol} \|x\|_2)$, with $\hat θ_k = \max(θ_k, θ_{k-1})$ and the rate $θ_k = \|Δ_k\|_2 / \|Δ_{k-1}\|_2$ of the step $Δ_k$.

Options{R} names the options of every norm in R.

The stopping norm is the norm of the residual test only. With norm = MaxNorm() the residual test reads $\|F(x)\|_\infty \le \max(f_{abstol}, f_{reltol} \|F(x₀)\|_\infty)$, so the default f_reltol is relative to the max norm of the start. The step test, the stationarity test, the NONFINITE test of the start, the merit $\|F\|_2^2$ of every line search and status.fnorm stay in the 2-norm, so a SUCCESS under MaxNorm() can report a status.fnorm above f_abstol. On a Deflated problem the test reads the norm of the inner residual, $\|M F\|_N / M$.

The default residual test is relative. The residual cannot fall below about $\mathrm{eps}(T) \|J\| \|x\|$, whatever the precision of the residual (Tisseur 2001, Cor. 2.5), and a default cannot know $\|J\| \|x\|$. So the default f_abstol is 0, which turns the absolute test off, and the relative test $f_{reltol} \|F(x₀)\|$ decides.

The stopping test runs before the first step, and min_iterations is 0. From a start at the limiting accuracy one Newton step makes the result worse (Wilkinson, quoted by Tisseur 2001). The relative test cannot see such a start: before the first step it compares $\|F(x₀)\|$ with a fraction of itself. So a start at the limiting accuracy returns unchanged after 0 iterations only when the caller sets f_abstol at or above its residual floor, as an integrator that knows the scale of F can.

The rate test joins the step test (Hairer and Wanner 1996, Solving Ordinary Differential Equations II, Sec. IV.8, (8.9)–(8.10), doi:10.1007/978-3-642-05221-7). From the second step on, the rate of the step $Δ_k$ is $θ_k = \|Δ_k\|_2 / \|Δ_{k-1}\|_2$, and once the residual test fails, a step with $\hat θ_k = \max(θ_k, θ_{k-1}) < 1$ and $\hat θ_k / (1 - \hat θ_k) \|Δ_k\|_2 ≤ κ \max(x_{abstol}, x_{reltol} \|x\|_2)$ returns SUCCESS with status.contracted == true. The left side estimates the distance of x to the limit of a contraction of rate $\hat θ_k$. The test reads only a step of full length: a step that a line search cut below the full step (shortened in its StepInfo) has a small rate because the line search shortened it, not because the iteration contracts, so it passes no rate test. The larger of the last two rates keeps the one small rate of an irregular step of full length, such as a secant step or an inexact Krylov step, from ending a solve. The rate is NaN at the first step and after a step of zero norm, and a NaN rate passes no test, so the rate test holds at the third step at the earliest; status.rate is $θ_k$. The test reads only a solve whose last fresh factor reports no rank (status.rank == -1, as an LU or a QR factor does) or full column rank: along a rank-deficient SVD factor the steps can contract onto a point that is no root, and the step test decides. On a LeastSquaresProblem, a VariableProjection and a Reparametrized problem around a LeastSquaresProblem it reads no step along a reused factor of reuse = k: such steps contract onto the point where $J_0^\top F(x) = 0$ for the Jacobian $J_0$ of the factor, which is no stationary point of $\|F\|_2^2$, so the stationarity, the step or the residual test ends the solve there, as without reuse. A step along a fresh factor, and every step of a square problem, reads the rate test. With rate_test = false no step reads it, and the residual, the stationarity and the step test end the solve as they would without it. A caller whose own schedule needs the residual tolerance turns it off: the inner solves of a BatchEliminated problem do, because the rate test stops at a residual above that tolerance.

The round-off stop is off by default. With roundoff = true, the first step of rate $θ_k ≥ 1$ after a step of rate $θ_{k-1} < 1$ returns STALLED with the iterate it reached (Hairer, McLachlan and Razakarivony 2008, BIT Numer. Math. 48, p. 235, doi:10.1007/s10543-008-0170-3): the increments have stopped decreasing, as they do once the residual reaches its round-off floor. A step of zero norm returns STALLED under either setting, as solve!! says.

The rate test and the round-off stop read the steps that the step test reads: not a STALLED step, not a step that a radius or a damping limits, and not before min_iterations steps. The rate test also reads no step that a line search shortened.

A negative or NaN tolerance, a negative min_iterations, a max_iterations below min_iterations, a max_stalls below 1 and a kappa outside $(0, 1)$ in real(T) raise an ArgumentError.

SciMLBase extension

When SciMLBase is loaded, the package extension GeometricSolversSciMLBaseExt adds methods of solve and init for a SciMLBase.NonlinearProblem and a method Newton or Picard. Both translate the problem and call the native solve or init, so solve returns (x, status), init returns a NonlinearSolver that holds a copy of an array u0, and solve!(init(prob, method; kwargs...)) returns what solve(prob, method; kwargs...) returns; the native solve! serves the solver. A SciML caller maps status.code to a SciMLBase.ReturnCode.T with sciml_retcode (GeometricSolvers.sciml_retcode(status.code)), which is public and not exported.

The call form follows SciMLBase.isinplace(prob) and u0 = prob.u0: an out-of-place f(u, p) with a StaticArray or a Number u0 solves in R1 and returns a value of the type of u0 (with an SVector u0, an out-of-place jac(u, p) must return an SMatrix, as the native R1 J does); an in-place f(du, u, p) with an array u0 that is not a StaticArray solves in R3 on a copy of u0; an out-of-place f(u, p) with such an array u0 solves in R3 through a closure wrapper (y, x, p) -> (y .= f(x, p)) (and (A, x, p) -> (A .= jac(x, p)) for prob.f.jac), which allocates as f and jac do; and an in-place f with a StaticArray or a Number u0 raises an ArgumentError. prob.f.jac becomes the J of the native problem (overridden by a method's own jacobian field), and prob.p is passed unchanged, SciMLBase.NullParameters() included. A problem with jac_prototype, sparsity, lb or ub set raises an ArgumentError.

The extension merges prob.kwargs with the call's keywords (the call's winning) and accepts the closed set abstol, reltol, maxiters and options: abstol maps to f_abstol (and sets f_reltol = 0 when reltol is not given), reltol maps to f_reltol, maxiters maps to max_iterations, and options passes an Options alone; any other keyword, or options together with another keyword, raises an ArgumentError.

IntervalArithmetic extension

When IntervalArithmetic is loaded, the package extension GeometricSolversIntervalArithmeticExt adds a method of certify for a NonlinearProblem and an iterate x that is a real floating-point Number, a StaticVector or a host Vector. It runs Krawczyk's test in intervals of the element type of x and returns (certified, enclosure, guaranteed): whether a box around x holds exactly one root, an enclosure of that root, and whether IntervalArithmetic guarantees the intervals of F(x) and J(X). certify is public and not exported (GeometricSolvers.certify); without the extension it has no method.

GeometricSolvers.certify — Function
certify(prob::NonlinearProblem, x, p = nothing) -> (; certified, enclosure, guaranteed)

Test with interval arithmetic whether prob has a root near the iterate x, for the parameters p, when IntervalArithmetic is loaded. The package extension GeometricSolversIntervalArithmeticExt adds the method; without it, certify has no method.

The test is Krawczyk's: with Y the inverse of the Jacobian of F at x, and a box X around x, the operator

$K(X) = x - Y F(x) + (I - Y J(X)) (X - x)$

is evaluated in intervals. When K(X) lies in the interior of X, the box X holds exactly one root of F, and K(X) encloses it. The first box has the radius of one eps of x, and each failed test widens the box to twice the distance of K(X) from x, plus one eps of x, up to 10 boxes.

The result is a NamedTuple of one concrete type for both outcomes:

  • certified: whether the test holds.
  • enclosure: K(X) when certified, in the storage of x with entries Interval{T} for the element type T of x: an Interval{T} for a Number, an SVector or an MVector of them for an SVector or an MVector, and a Vector of them for a Vector. With no certificate, every entry is the whole real line.
  • guaranteed: whether every interval of F(x) and J(X) is guaranteed, that is, carries no NG flag of IntervalArithmetic. A number that is not an interval, such as the 1 of 1 - x[1], sets the flag where it meets an interval, and the certificate holds without the guarantee. With no certificate, guaranteed is false.

Every hypothesis of the test is read from F: ForwardDiff differentiates F for Y and for J(X), and the J of the problem is not read. The call form of F follows the storage of x, as for a solve: F(x, p) for a real floating-point Number and a StaticVector, and F(y, x, p) for a host Vector. F must accept floats, intervals and ForwardDiff duals of both. An error of F is not caught: log of a negative float x throws a DomainError, and an F that branches on a comparison of intervals, such as x[1] < 0, or that calls max or min on arguments whose order the box does not fix, throws IntervalArithmetic's InconclusiveBooleanOperation where the comparison has no single answer.

A singular Jacobian at x, a non-finite x or Y, a box radius that is not finite, an unbounded box X, an unbounded x - Y F(x) or I - Y J(X), and a test that fails on the tenth box each give certified == false. So does an interval of F(x), F(X) or J(X) that is unbounded, or that IntervalArithmetic decorates below dac (defined and continuous): F can then be undefined, not continuous or not continuously differentiable on part of the box, such as log or sqrt across 0, 1/x at 0, floor across an integer, or a kink of abs. The decoration follows the functions that F calls, so abs across 0 refuses also where F is continuously differentiable, such as x * abs(x) + x at 0. None of these throws. A complex iterate, a batch, a device array, a StationarityProblem and a LeastSquaresProblem have no method.

certify is public and not exported.
