Mixed precision

A solve has up to three precisions:

precisionholdsset by
working precision Tthe iterate, the residual, the Jacobian buffer, the stopping test and the statusthe element type of x₀
factorisation precision TFthe factorisation and the triangular solvesthe type parameter of the linear method
refinement residual$-F(x) - J d$ in a refinement passT

The working precision sets the attainable accuracy of Newton's method, because the residual and the update are computed in T. The factorisation precision changes the rate of convergence, not the limit (Kelley 2022; Higham and Mary 2022).

The factorisation precision is a type parameter

LUFactorization(Float32) is LUFactorization{Float32}(), and the same holds for LDLTFactorization, QRFactorization and SVDFactorization. The default LUFactorization() is LUFactorization{Nothing}(), which factorises in T. For a complex problem the precision stays real: LUFactorization(Float32) factorises a ComplexF64 Jacobian in ComplexF32. factor_eltype gives the element type of the factors:

julia> using GeometricSolvers

julia> GeometricSolvers.factor_eltype(LUFactorization(Float32), ComplexF64) === ComplexF32
true

julia> GeometricSolvers.factor_eltype(LUFactorization(), Float64)
Float64

The precision is a type parameter and not a field, so the method stays isbits and enters a kernel, and the factor type that init_linear builds carries both the precision and the backend, which selects any vendor method by dispatch.

The scaled mixed-precision solve

The boundary between the two precisions is one method of ldiv!!, the one for a factor whose element type differs from that of the right-hand side (src/linear/mixed.jl):

  1. factorize!! divides the matrix by a power of two $2^e$ near its largest entry, converts it to TF, and factorises. A power of two is exact in every binary format, so the scaling adds no rounding error, and the largest entries of a Float64 matrix stay in the Float32 range. One factor of scale does not cover an arbitrary dynamic range: an entry far below the largest one underflows in TF, and the factor can then be singular, as for $\operatorname{diag}(2^{128}, 2^{-128})$. An LU, LDLᵀ or QR factor records the zero pivot, and the remedy chain then refactorises in T. An SVD factor drops the direction and records no failure, so the chain refactorises in T only after a failed refinement or two slow steps, and the solve can end on STALLED before that.
  2. ldiv!! scales the right-hand side by $s = \|b\|_\infty$, converts it to TF, and solves. The triangular solve in TF can overflow where the solution in T is finite: for $\operatorname{diag}(1, 2^{-128})$ and $b = (0, 1)$, the step $2^{128}$ exceeds floatmax(Float32).
  3. The solution is multiplied by $s \cdot 2^{-e}$ in the wider of the two precisions and only then converted to T, so this rescaling adds no overflow or underflow beyond that of the solution itself.

The bound that the test suite verifies for this solve is on the page Numerical Stability and Backward-Error Bounds.

julia> F!(y, x, p) = (y[1] = x[1]^2 + x[2]^2 - p; y[2] = x[1] - x[2]; y);

julia> prob = NonlinearProblem(F!);

julia> solver = init(prob, [1.0, 0.5], Newton(; linsolve = LUFactorization(Float32)), 2.0);

julia> x, status = solve!(solver);

julia> status.code === SUCCESS && x ≈ [1.0, 1.0]
true

julia> eltype(stored_factors(solver))
Float32

stored_factors returns the packed L and U of the last factorisation in TF.

Refinement: NoRefinement, Refinement, GMRESRefinement

The refinement field of Newton corrects the linear direction in T before the globalization:

  • NoRefinement (the default) takes the direction of the solve in TF as it is. The step is then an inexact Newton step, and the rate slows towards linear as the error of the solve grows.
  • Refinement(T; η, maxiter) is the LU-IR loop: it forms the residual $r = -F(x) - J d$ in T, solves for a correction with the factor in TF, and stops when $\|F(x) + J d\| \le \max(\eta, \sqrt{\varepsilon(T)})\,\|F(x)\|$, or with a failure when a pass does not decrease that norm. The theory of iterative refinement in three precisions is that of Carson and Higham (2018).
  • GMRESRefinement(T; η, maxiter, gmres_maxiter, gmres_tol) runs the same passes first and, where they fail, a GMRES in T left-preconditioned by the factors promoted to T: the GMRES-IR variant SDD of Amestoy, Buttari, Higham, L'Excellent, Mary and Vieublé (2024). The promoted copy is promoted_factors.
julia> for ref in (Refinement(), GMRESRefinement())
           method = Newton(; linsolve = LUFactorization(Float32), refinement = ref)
           x, status = solve(prob, [1.0, 0.5], method, 2.0)
           println(status.code, " ", x ≈ [1.0, 1.0])
       end
SUCCESS true
SUCCESS true

The remedy chain

A step whose factorisation in TF fails, a step whose refinement stages all fail, or two consecutive steps whose linear model is inaccurate in TF and which lower the residual by less than half, make the solve refactorise the Jacobian in T. The stages of Refinement are the LU-IR passes; GMRESRefinement runs GMRES-IR after them, so it refactorises in T only when GMRES-IR fails too. The chain of a step is therefore

LU-IR  →  GMRES-IR (with GMRESRefinement)  →  refactorisation in T

SolverStatus counts each failed stage in step_failures and records a factorisation in T in promoted. A refinement drives the linear residual $J d + F$ to zero, so it does not apply to a LeastSquaresProblem with more residuals than unknowns, nor to the damped LevenbergMarquardt step; a GMRES solve has no factor to refine with.

A value iterate (Number, SVector) has no Krylov basis, so a GMRESRefinement there runs the LU-IR passes alone.

When a lower factorisation precision pays

A factorisation in TF below T saves time and memory only where the factorisation dominates the cost of a step, that is where the evaluation of F and J costs less than the $O(n^3)$ factorisation (Kelley 2022). In R1 the gain is registers and memory, not operations.

Its applicability depends on the condition of the Jacobian. Without refinement, Newton's method converges while the error of the solve in TF, roughly $\kappa(J)\,\varepsilon(T_F)$ times a modest function of n, stays below one (Higham and Mary 2022); the convergence slows as it grows. LU-IR converges under a condition of the same form and then recovers the iteration count of a solve in T (Carson and Higham 2018). GMRES-IR with promoted factors extends the range of condition numbers further (Amestoy et al. 2024). Where these conditions fail, the remedy chain above refactorises in T, and status.promoted records it.

The repository holds scripts/gmres_ir_comparison.jl, which compares the Newton iteration counts and the linear residuals of Refinement and GMRESRefinement over a range of condition numbers.

Mixed precision by backend

The pairs T = Float64 with TF = Float32 (and their complex forms) run on the CPU, on CUDA and on ROCm. On CUDA, a Float32 factor of a Float64 problem reaches the cuSOLVER method for Float32. Metal has no Float64, so T = Float32 there and the pair with TF = Float32 does not apply; see Device precision.

A residual can itself be less accurate than T, for instance when it calls a Float32 computation inside a Float64 solve. The limit of the solve is then the accuracy of F, and the tolerance must respect it: a tolerance below that floor ends on STALLED. The BatchEliminated problem shows this with a Float32 batch inside a Float64 outer solve.
