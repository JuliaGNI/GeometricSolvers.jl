Globalization

A globalization decides how far a step goes along the direction of the method. The package has two kinds:

  • line searches (Static, Backtracking, Bisection, StrongWolfe) search along the direction d on the merit $\varphi(\alpha) = \|F(x + \alpha d)\|_2^2$. They serve Newton and Picard.
  • trust-region and damped steps (DogLeg, LevenbergMarquardt, PseudoTransient for a StationarityProblem) change the direction itself and need the Jacobian and its factor. They serve Newton only, which keeps the radius, the damping parameter or the pseudo-time step in its state.

A line search is scalar code: linesearch sees the line only through $\varphi$ and its derivative $\varphi'$, allocates nothing and never throws, so the same search runs on the host and inside a kernel. The slope $\varphi'(0)$ of a Newton direction is known exactly ($-2\varphi(0)$ for an exact step); for an inexact or a Picard step it is one Jacobian–vector product. The iteration cap of a search is its own maxiter field.

The example of this page is $F(x) = \arctan(x)$ from $x_0 = 3$, where the full Newton step overshoots and the iterates grow:

julia> using GeometricSolvers

julia> prob = NonlinearProblem((x, p) -> atan(x));

julia> solve(prob, 3.0, Newton())[2].code === SUCCESS
false

Static

Static(α = 1.0) takes the fixed step α and evaluates nothing. It is the default and the common case inside an integrator, where the start is close to the solution.

Backtracking

Backtracking(; c₁ = 1e-4, p = 0.5, maxiter = 100) is the Armijo line search with safeguarded quadratic and cubic interpolation. It shrinks the trial step until the sufficient decrease condition $\varphi(\alpha) - \varphi(0) \le c_1 \alpha \varphi'(0)$ holds, with a round-off allowance. For an inexact step it tests the decrease of the residual norm against the linear residual of the step instead. It needs one residual evaluation per trial.

julia> x, status = solve(prob, 3.0, Newton(; globalization = Backtracking()));

julia> status.code === SUCCESS && abs(x) < 1e-8
true

Bisection

Bisection(; αmax = 65536.0, maxiter = 100) brackets a sign change of $\varphi'$ from $-$ to $+$ and bisects it, so a bracketed search returns a minimiser of the merit along the line, never a maximiser. Where $\varphi'$ stays negative up to αmax, it returns the step αmax, and after maxiter trials it ends with LINESEARCH_FAILED. It costs one $\varphi'$ per trial: one for each doubling of the trial step while $\varphi'$ stays negative, then one for each bisection. While the lower end of the bracket is $0$, the bisections grow with $\log_2 \log_2$ of the ratio of the trial step to the step floor; a bracket with a positive lower end is bisected to a relative width of $\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. It can take a step longer than the trial step.

julia> x, status = solve(prob, 3.0, Newton(; globalization = Bisection()));

julia> status.code === SUCCESS && abs(x) < 1e-8
true

StrongWolfe

StrongWolfe(; c₁ = 1e-4, c₂ = 0.9, αmax = 65536.0, maxiter = 100) finds a step that satisfies the strong Wolfe conditions

\[\varphi(\alpha) - \varphi(0) \le c_1 \alpha \varphi'(0), \qquad |\varphi'(\alpha)| \le c_2 |\varphi'(0)|,\]

in a bracketing phase, which doubles the trial step, and a zoom phase, which interpolates inside the bracket. A trial that fails the first condition costs one $\varphi$, and a trial that passes it one $\varphi$ and one $\varphi'$.

julia> x, status = solve(prob, 3.0, Newton(; globalization = StrongWolfe()));

julia> status.code === SUCCESS && abs(x) < 1e-8
true

DogLeg

DogLeg(; radius = nothing) is the trust-region globalization on the dogleg curve of Pawlowski, Simonis, Walker and Shadid (2008). The step is the Newton step inside the radius, the Cauchy step when the Cauchy point lies outside it, and otherwise the point of the radius on the segment between them. A rejected trial shrinks the radius and chooses again on the same curve, without a new Jacobian or factorisation. A singular factor gives the Cauchy step, so DogLeg continues where a line search along a Newton direction has no direction.

julia> x, status = solve(prob, 3.0, Newton(; globalization = DogLeg()));

julia> status.code === SUCCESS && abs(x) < 1e-8
true

With a GMRES linear solver, DogLeg runs on the Arnoldi relation of one unrestarted cycle without a preconditioner.

LevenbergMarquardt

LevenbergMarquardt(R = Float64; μ₀, μ_min, μ_max, μ_decrease, μ_increase, ρ_accept, scaling) computes the trial step from the damped model $(J^\mathsf{H} J + \mu D^\mathsf{T} D)\, \delta = -J^\mathsf{H} F$ and adapts $\mu$ from the gain ratio of the actual and the predicted reduction. A rejected trial raises $\mu$ and solves again, without a new Jacobian. With SVDFactorization and scaling = :none each trial reuses one SVD of $J$; with the other factorisations, or with scaling = :diagonal, the stacked path reduces [J | -F] once per step and each trial eliminates only its own damping rows by Givens rotations, so $J^\mathsf{H} J$ is never formed. It serves a NonlinearProblem, a StationarityProblem and a LeastSquaresProblem.

julia> x, status = solve(prob, 3.0, Newton(; globalization = LevenbergMarquardt()));

julia> status.code === SUCCESS && abs(x) < 1e-8
true

scaling = :diagonal scales the damping by the column norms of $J$. The stacked path indexes scalars and runs on a host array only. LevenbergMarquardt takes no refinement and no factor reuse.

How to choose

  • Close to the solution, as in the stages of an implicit integrator, Static costs nothing beyond the Newton step.
  • From a distant start, a line search or a trust region makes the merit decrease. A line search needs only residual evaluations and slopes along one direction. DogLeg and LevenbergMarquardt change the direction and continue where the Jacobian is singular or ill-conditioned.
  • For a least-squares problem, LevenbergMarquardt damps the Gauss–Newton step.
  • On a device array, the stacked LevenbergMarquardt path is refused with an ArgumentError, because it indexes scalars: only SVDFactorization with scaling = :none runs there.

KNOWN_ISSUES.md in the repository lists the problems where a globalization is known to fail.
